# Data Plan

Core data is user input stored by the artifact's own actions: profiles (name, phone, home county/district/city/community picked from built-in Liberia lists, profile photo, ID photo, Mobile Money number, work preferences) and jobs (service, exact task, the work's own question answers, the place from the poster's profile, Mobile Money payment).

Built-in reference data in the client (no external fetch): the Liberia place data in `client/src/places.ts`, generated from the real sourced file `~/workspace/jackwork/data/liberia_places.json` (14,064 records; build notes in `liberia_places_notes.md` beside it) plus the real communities files `~/workspace/jackwork/data/liberia_communities_v2.json` (183 records; coverage report in `communities_v2_report.md` beside it) and `~/workspace/jackwork/data/liberia_communities_v3.json` (113 records; coverage report in `communities_v3_report.md` beside it) — 14,360 source records in total — and the service catalog in `client/src/App.tsx`. The catalog is the owner's own supplied list (Liberia's version of TaskRabbit), ordered by his top-10 demand order (Cleaning, Handyman, Plumbing, Electrical, Moving & Delivery, Mechanic Services, Generator, Beauty, Tutoring, Construction) and broken down into 400+ specific jobs written in problem words people actually use ("Unblock toilet", "Generator won't start"), each service with its own short detail questions whose depth lives in richer tap-answer options on ONE optional details screen. Childcare and caregiving stay their own kind of work; Premium doctors are appointment booking only. Prices are never in the app (a separate sheet is the owner's to approve later); the price a poster offers is entered on the Mobile Money pay screen in Liberian dollars (LD, listed first and default) or USD, and jobs display amounts in the currency picked. The owner's two slogans are deliberately NOT shown anywhere in the UI (no extra text rule).

No external images or maps. Post a job never asks location and shows no map: the job's place is taken automatically from the poster's profile. The only pictures in the app are a person's own profile picture and their ID photo.

## Scheduling (added 2026-11-11 at the owner's order)

No vague time stretches anywhere in Post a job (no "today / this week / next week" choices, and the detail questions no longer ask them either). Every non-emergency job stores the exact day and time the poster picked: `jobs.scheduled_date` (YYYY-MM-DD) and `jobs.scheduled_time` (e.g. "9:00 AM"), picked on two required screens — a day picker of the next 14 real days labeled with day name and date ("Friday, Nov 14"), then a time picker of exact times on that day (times already past on a picked today are not shown, and posting re-checks the moment is still future). `jobs.urgency` mirrors the formatted moment ("Friday, Nov 14 at 9:00 AM") for older displays. The exact moment is shown on the Check screen, the job screen, My jobs, and the Find work board. Need It NOW (emergency) jobs are the one exception: they store no day/time and show "Right now".

## Address flow: STEPPED place picker, search at each level (2026-11-12 at the owner's order, replacing the one search the same day)

At sign-up (and in Profile place editing) the place is picked in steps, one screen per level, each level with its own search box that searches only that level: first "What county are you in?" (the 15 counties, ABC order with Montserrado pinned at the top), then "What district are you in?" (only that county's districts), then "What city or town are you in?" (only that district's towns), then "What community are you in?" (only that town's communities, and only when the town has any; a town with no communities finishes at the town pick). Tapping a pick moves straight to the next level, never back to the level just picked. History: the stepped lists were the settled way from 2026-11-11; on 2026-11-12 the owner's mother said the long lists are too much and the person should just type it and have it come up, and the owner ordered one single search over all levels together; testing that search himself the same day, he found choosing a county did not move him on and kept making him choose the county again, and he ordered the stepped flow back, with the forgiving search kept inside each level. So each level's search is forgiving on purpose: results start from just two letters typed; matching is normalized (case, spaces, punctuation, doubled letters folded), prefix and substring matches count, and every name also carries a soundex-style sound key (ph/f, c/k, s/z, qu/kw folded), so a name spelled the way it sounds still finds the real place, and a near-miss edit distance catches the rest; best matches first (exact, then starts-with, then same sound, then word matches), capped at 12. What is saved is unchanged: the full fixed database entry (county, district, town/city, community where picked, coordinates), through `updateHomeLocation`, which requires a city, unchanged — search text is never saved, and picks are still database records only, never typed text. One honest consequence of the steps: the Brewerville, Garwolon, and Johnsonville community records sit under towns that have no town record of their own in the sourced data, so those communities cannot be reached through the town level, exactly as their records stand; no town point was invented for them (a prepared town points patch exists but stays unloaded until the owner says yes). Then one last screen: the house description below. That is the whole address.

The only person-specific part of an address is the person's own house: one short description in their own words ("When you get to the field, call me", a landmark near the house), written once and kept with their place on the profile in `profiles.house_directions`. It is not an address form, not a map, not pins, and Post a job adds NO location steps: every job copies it automatically from the poster's profile into `jobs.house_directions`, and the worker who accepted the job (and the poster) sees it on the job. The whole path stays: fixed community pick for filtering and distance, one short house description in plain words. There is no call number on a job: contact between the two people is in-app chat and in-app voice calls (see the contact section below).

## Places are the REAL sourced data, nothing invented (loaded 2026-11-11 at the owner's order)

The only places a person can pick are records from `~/workspace/jackwork/data/liberia_places.json` plus `~/workspace/jackwork/data/liberia_communities_v2.json` plus `~/workspace/jackwork/data/liberia_communities_v3.json`, generated into `client/src/places.ts` (do not hand-edit names there). Sources: county/district names and every town with coordinates from HDX "Liberia - Settlements" (NIMAC / UNMIL / UNDP via OCHA, Dec 2017) joined to the official HDX COD-AB boundaries — 15 counties, 136 districts, 13,899 surveyed settlement points (13,536 unique town names; same-name points surveyed more than once in one district are one pick). Communities for Greater Monrovia come from "Communities of the Greater Monrovia District" (Wikipedia compilation of LISGIS 2014 estimates / NEC): 165 named communities under 12 towns, with no coordinates of their own (a community pick uses its town's surveyed coordinates; Garwolon and Johnsonville have no surveyed town point and get no distance). Communities outside Greater Monrovia were loaded 2026-11-11 from `liberia_communities_v2.json`: 183 real records under 20 towns in 13 counties (Gbarnga, Harper, Sanniquellie, Robertsport, Buchanan, Bleebo City for Pleebo, Greenville, and 13 more), each record naming its own source in that file (GeoNames sections of populated places, OpenStreetMap suburb/neighbourhood/quarter tags, published town community lists); 74 carry coordinates in the source file and 109 have none published — either way a community pick is held exactly like the Greater Monrovia ones, on its town's surveyed coordinates, and no coordinate is ever invented. Round 3 communities were loaded 2026-11-12 from `liberia_communities_v3.json`: 113 more real records under 18 towns (NEC electoral district map labels georeferenced against known points, the LEC Kakata network inspection report, township lists, and town-by-town digging; every record names its own source and strength in that file — 46 strong, 67 weak, loaded identically, strength is provenance bookkeeping only; 38 carry coordinates in the source file and 75 publish none, and no coordinate was added where the file has null). First community level for Bopolu (3), Foya (3), Bensonville (2), Careysburg (4), Virginia (4), Brewerville (10), Zorzor (3), Bong Town (1), and Suakoko (1); Kakata deepened by 31 (now 37), Zwedru by 18 (now 22), Ganta by 16 (now 19), Tubmanburg by 6 (now 11). The v3 file writes five of those towns by their common city names, so its rows are keyed to each town's own settlement record using the research pipeline's documented equivalences (Foya = Foya City, Virginia = Central Virginia, Bong Town = Bong mines, Suakoko = Suacoco, Harbel = Harbel City, including Harbel's district following the settlement record, Firestone not the file's Mambah Kaba) — community names are the file's byte-for-byte, only the join follows the settlement record, exactly how the earlier loads keyed their rows. Brewerville has no surveyed settlement point of its own in the HDX data, so its 10 rows are loaded keyed as the file has them with no town point, held exactly like Garwolon and Johnsonville. Harbel's 2 records join the existing Harbel City key, so the 461 records sit under 41 town keys in the data (42 towns counted in the source files). In total 461 community records now carry a community level in the picker. The separate clans file (`liberia_clans.json`) is NOT loaded. The earlier hand-written town lists and ALL generated zone names (town + "Central / Old Town / Market Area / Mission Area / New Town") and the old hand-written community lists were removed completely — if a name is not in a real file, it cannot be picked. A town with no sourced community list keeps `community` null by design, and a town pick there is complete by itself with no community step. Picking a town that has communities offers its real communities under it, plus the town itself.

Home location is picked from this data only — through the stepped picker described above, each level searched with the forgiving matcher over that level's names — with NO free-text entry for town or community: typed words only find records, and only a tapped record is saved. The one free-text field in the whole address is the house description, never used for filtering. What saves is the full record: county, district, town, community where present, plus its coordinates (`profiles.place_lat/place_lng`, migration 0011; jobs copy theirs into `jobs.place_lat/place_lng` at posting). The picked community is stored as its own column, `profiles.community` (mirrored into `profiles.address` for older displays), identical for everyone in that community. Jobs carry the poster's picked community (in `jobs.landmark`, and in the job description). Find work orders nearest first by the real distance between the worker's and the job's place coordinates and shows it plainly in km ("33 km away"); places with no coordinates fall back to the fixed-place levels (same community, town, district, county) and the "In your community / In your town / In your district / In your county" labels. Profiles/jobs saved before this load keep their saved words, are matched by exact name to the real records for distance when possible, and otherwise fall back to town-level display with no distance — nothing crashes.

## Admin panel REMOVED, Call in stripped to a number, red selection highlight (2026-11-11 at the owner's order)

There is NO admin panel in this app anymore: the start-screen Admin entry, the whole AdminFlow area (profile/job data views, place filters and counts, verify/delete controls), and the admin-only server actions (`adminSetVerification`, `adminDeleteProfile`) are all deleted. `listAllProfiles` stays only because the Find work board reads worker photos and picked places from it for job cards; it has no admin UI. Call in is just there: a small plain "Call in" on the sign-in and start screens (no "can't use the app" wording anywhere), opening one plain screen. The number is the single `CALL_IN_NUMBER` constant at the top of `client/src/App.tsx`, currently empty (no number set yet). While the number is unset, the screen shows only a short detail in plain words — Call in is not available yet; later you will be able to call Jackwork, and a person will answer, tell you what to do, or do it for you — with no empty number area. When a number is set later, the big number and Call button appear above that same detail. Selection highlight: every picked state (calendar day fill, time hour/minute/AM-PM taps, detail-question answers, LRD/USD currency pick, Find work category chips) now fills with the `--select` token in `theme.css` — a smooth deep red (#8e2f2a on black with a soft red glow, #b4453c softened on white), white text on top — instead of the old white/light fill. Nothing else changed color: primary buttons and the rest of the app stay black and white.

## Background design gradient is red (2026-11-11 at the owner's order)

The soft gradient glow behind everything on every screen (the `body::before` washes in `theme.css` and the matching root background in `App.tsx`) is built from four gradient stop tokens, `--glow-1` to `--glow-4`, holding PLAIN rgb-with-alpha colors in the same smoothing red family as the selection highlight: `rgb(142 47 42 / 42%, 32%, 22%, 19%)` on black in dark mode, and `rgb(180 69 60 / 55%, 44%, 32%, 26%)` in light mode. Do NOT rebuild these stops with `color-mix()`: the stops were color-mix expressions until 2026-11-11, and on the owner's real phone the light-mode background rendered with NO red at all (flat white) while dark mode showed it — an engine that cannot resolve color-mix drops the whole background declaration, and the only other difference between the modes was the washed-out light alphas (originally 16/12/8/7%, which even where they rendered read as near-white on a phone screen). The fix removed color-mix from both gradient definitions entirely and set the light stops strong enough to plainly wash red over white, with black text fully readable on top; the dark stops hold the exact colors the old color-mix values produced, so dark mode renders unchanged. Color only; the gradient's shape, positions, and softness are unchanged in both modes. The selection highlight itself is unchanged; glass panels and all other colors stay black and white.

The word "JackWork" (capital J, capital W) from the owner's finished logo sits in the top right corner, a little down from the top edge, on the entry screens only: the three sign-in screens (name, phone, code) and the signed-in start screen (Post a job / Find work / My jobs). The wordmark no longer renders as solid print on top of the screen. ## Privacy hardening from the same audit (2026-11-11)

The audit's security review required, and this build adds: (1) Sign-in now issues a session token (`profiles.session_token`, migration 0012, rotated at every code sign-in, kept on the device next to the phone). `getMyProfile` returns a profile ONLY to the holder of that profile's token — a phone number alone returns nothing — and the profile payload no longer contains the ID number or ID photo at all; the person's own My ID screen reads those through the session-checked `getMyIdDocument` action (number shown only as its last 3 digits). Anyone signed in before this change signs in once more with a fresh code to get a token. (2) `listJobs` is viewer-aware, with the viewer derived server-side from the caller's session token (never from a caller-supplied id): house directions and the poster's own GPS pin are included only when the token's owner posted that job or accepted it; everyone else gets the public board view. Phone numbers go further (2026-11-12): each side's payload carries only its OWN numbers, so the worker's payload holds no poster number at all and the poster's payload holds no worker number, and no screen prints another person's digits. `listWorkers` likewise carries no phone numbers, only browsing fields (name, category, location, bio, rating). The town place coordinates stay in the board view on purpose: they are the public surveyed settlement points from the HDX dataset, identical for everyone in that town, and the board's distance feature runs on them. (3) `listAllProfiles` returns only board-safe cards (id, profile photo, picked place), as recorded above.

## Notifications (2026-11-12 at the owner's order)

Every job event writes one notification row for the person it concerns (accepted → poster, confirmed after the call → poster, price proposed → poster with the real amount, price accepted or declined → worker, Mobile Money marked sent → worker, worker started → poster, worker done → poster, job completed → worker with the payout line, Problem → the other side, including safety-valve auto completions). The start screen carries one plain "Notifications" item under "Call in"; the list is newest first, one line each, with a red dot on unseen items, and opening the list marks them seen on the server; tapping one opens that job. The same one line also goes out as a Web Push: VAPID keys are Worker secrets (never in the repo), subscriptions live in `push_subscriptions`, the client subscribes from the Notifications screen only ("Turn on phone notifications", service worker at /sw.js), and a 404 or 410 from the push service deletes the subscription. Text messages are built but waiting: the Worker holds SMS_PROVIDER, SMS_API_KEY, and SMS_SENDER_ID constants, all unset, so while no SMS line is connected every notification records `sms_status = 'waiting_for_line'`, no text is sent, and nothing anywhere claims one was. The sign-in code flow is untouched.

## Contact inside the app: chat and voice calls (2026-11-12 at the owner's order)

Normal phone calling is out: it shows people's numbers and it costs money, and Jackwork must be cheap and pay nothing per call. Once a worker accepts a job, the job's two people get a Chat inside the job's screens and can call each other inside the app. Chat lives in the `messages` table (id, job_id, sender_profile_id, body, created_at); the list and send actions are token-checked so only the job's two parties can read or write, the client polls like the rest of the app, and chat stays available for the job's whole life including after done. Messages are kept with the job, and when a Problem is reported the admin data for that job includes its chat so the operator can read it to help settle the problem (stated in the Privacy Policy). Voice is WebRTC, browser to browser over the internet: signaling rows in `call_signals` (ring, offer, answer, candidate, accept, decline, end) are polled by both clients while a call is being set up, a ring also writes a notification (and push) for the other side, and the call itself never touches the server. Only a free public STUN server is configured; there is NO TURN server, so on strict mobile networks some calls may not connect until a TURN server is added later, with Chat as the fallback, and the app says so plainly when a call cannot start or connect. Posting no longer asks for a "phone number the worker should call" (one less step): new jobs store no per-job call number, and older jobs keep their stored value unused and undisplayed. History, same day: an intermediate step first hid numbers from all screens and payloads and handed the number to the Call button only at tap time through the phone dialer (commit 3e5f11b); the owner then ordered contact fully in-app, which replaced the dialer path entirely, so no `tel:` link to a user number remains anywhere in the job flow and no number is handed out at all. A person's profile phone number remains the account identity only, shown only to its owner.

## Terms and Privacy, with the first-time gate (2026-11-12 at the owner's order)

The texts live in `client/src/legal.ts` as data. Version 2 (November 12, 2026) split the terms into two complete sets, at the owner's order after his own sign-up test: at create account the person first picks Worker or User on one screen; the Worker pick shows the Worker Terms, the User pick shows the User Terms, and they agree to the set they picked. Both sets are complete and final, written plain from how Jackwork works (marketplace, independent workers, Mobile Money through the platform, money held and released when both sides confirm done, the 10 percent cut, ratings, proof, in-app chat and calls, numbers hidden, Problem freeze and admin review, ID verification, account rules, liability), with no "not complete yet" wording and no payment timing disclaimers. Each document opens in two layers: a short numbered list of the main points, a divider, then the full sections. Before a person can open their account for the first time, they must open the terms for their pick from the gate screen and tap "I agree" on the reading screen; there is no skip. The agreement is recorded server side (`profiles.terms_version`, `profiles.terms_agreed_at`, and `profiles.terms_role` holding "worker" or "user", migration 0018, set by the token-checked `agreeToTerms` action, which now requires the role). New people meet the gate right after their code is verified, before place picking; anyone already in the database who never agreed meets it once on their next sign-in, before the start screen. Profiles that agreed to the Version 1 combined terms stay agreed (their `terms_role` is null): the split does not trap them at the gate. The pick decides only which terms a person agreed to; one profile can still both post jobs and take jobs, and no feature is blocked by the pick. Profile carries read-only "Terms of Service" (opening the set the person agreed to; Worker Terms for a Version 1 agreement) and "Privacy Policy" rows that open the same reading screens with no re-agree. The Privacy Policy still states the honest testing truth about sign-in codes showing on screen because no text line is connected yet.
